# Earnings results and news

## Quarterly results

Listed companies report results every quarter. In India, companies must publish quarterly results within 45 days of the quarter's end (60 days for the final quarter of the financial year). India's financial year runs from April to March, so Q1 is April to June, and "FY26" means April 2025 to March 2026.

Key things to read in a results announcement:

- Revenue and profit growth, both quarter-on-quarter (QoQ) and year-on-year (YoY). YoY is more reliable because many businesses are seasonal.
- Margins: did operating margin expand or contract, and why?
- Guidance: what management expects for the coming quarters or year. Changes in guidance often move the stock more than the results themselves.
- Segment performance: which business lines or geographies grew or shrank.
- Order book or deal wins for project businesses such as IT services and capital goods.

## Expectations: beats and misses

Analysts publish estimates before results; the average is called the consensus. A "beat" means results came in above consensus and a "miss" means below. Stocks often move on the surprise relative to expectations rather than on whether the results were good in absolute terms. A company can report record profits and still fall if investors expected more.

## Earnings calls

After results, management usually holds a conference call (concall) with analysts. The question-and-answer section often reveals more than the press release: demand trends, pricing pressure, hiring plans and risks. Changes in tone between calls are worth noting.

## Reading news critically

- Separate facts (a contract was signed, a regulator imposed a penalty) from opinions (an analyst thinks the stock will rise).
- Check the date: old news is usually already reflected in the price.
- Ask whether the news changes long-term earnings, or only affects sentiment for a few days.
- Be careful with headlines that report price moves after the fact ("stock jumps 5%") - they describe, they don't explain.
- Prefer primary sources: exchange filings on NSE/BSE, company press releases and annual reports.

## News sentiment and its limits

Positive or negative news flow can push prices in the short term, but sentiment reverses quickly and much news is already priced in by the time it is widely reported. Many signals mixing positive and negative headlines usually point to a neutral or uncertain outlook rather than a clear direction.

## Analyst ratings and price targets

Brokerage analysts publish ratings (buy, hold, sell) and 12-month price targets. They are useful as a summary of professional opinion, but targets are frequently revised after the price moves, analysts can have conflicts of interest, and the average target has a poor record of predicting exact prices.

## Events that commonly move stocks

- Earnings results and guidance changes.
- Large contract wins or losses.
- Management changes, especially CEO or CFO exits.
- Regulatory actions, penalties or approvals.
- Mergers, acquisitions, demergers and buybacks.
- Index inclusion or exclusion (for example joining the Nifty 50), which forces index funds to buy or sell.
- Promoter buying, selling or pledging of shares.
- Macro events: RBI interest-rate decisions, the Union Budget, US Federal Reserve decisions, currency moves and commodity prices.
