# How the Indian stock market works

## Exchanges and indices

India has two main stock exchanges: the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and the BSE (formerly the Bombay Stock Exchange). Most large companies are listed on both. On Yahoo Finance, NSE tickers end in .NS (for example TCS.NS) and BSE tickers end in .BO (for example TCS.BO).

The two headline indices are the Nifty 50 (the 50 largest and most liquid NSE stocks, Yahoo symbol ^NSEI) and the Sensex (30 large BSE companies, Yahoo symbol ^BSESN). Sector indices such as Nifty IT and Nifty Bank track specific industries.

## The regulator: SEBI

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) regulates the securities markets. It protects investors, sets disclosure rules for listed companies, and registers market intermediaries. Anyone who gives personalised investment advice for a fee in India must be registered with SEBI as an Investment Adviser (RIA), and research analysts publishing buy/sell recommendations must be registered as Research Analysts (RA). Unregistered "stock tips" on social media are a common source of losses and fraud.

## Trading hours and settlement

Normal equity trading on NSE and BSE runs from 9:15 am to 3:30 pm IST, Monday to Friday, except exchange holidays. A pre-open session runs from 9:00 to 9:15 am to discover the opening price.

India moved to T+1 settlement in 2023: shares and money change hands one working day after the trade.

## Price bands and circuit limits

Exchanges set daily price bands (circuit limits) for individual stocks, commonly 2%, 5%, 10% or 20%, to slow down extreme moves. Market-wide circuit breakers pause all trading if the Nifty 50 or Sensex moves 10%, 15% or 20% in a day.

## Demat accounts and brokers

To buy shares, an investor needs a demat account (which holds shares electronically, through the depositories NSDL or CDSL) and a trading account with a SEBI-registered broker.

## Corporate actions

- Dividend: a cash payout to shareholders. To receive it you must hold the shares on the record date.
- Bonus issue: free extra shares (for example 1:1 doubles your share count); the share price adjusts down, so total value does not change.
- Stock split: one share is divided into several; again value is unchanged.
- Buyback: the company repurchases its own shares, often at a premium, reducing share count.
- Rights issue: existing shareholders may buy new shares, usually at a discount.

## Taxes on listed shares (as announced in the July 2024 Union Budget)

- Short-term capital gains (shares held 12 months or less): taxed at 20%.
- Long-term capital gains (held more than 12 months): gains above Rs 1.25 lakh per year are taxed at 12.5%.
- Securities Transaction Tax (STT) applies to every equity trade.
- Dividends are added to income and taxed at the investor's slab rate.

Tax rules change with each budget, so always check the current rules or a tax professional.

## Who moves the market

Foreign portfolio investors (FPIs/FIIs), domestic institutional investors (mutual funds, insurers, pension funds) and retail investors all influence prices. Monthly SIP (systematic investment plan) inflows into mutual funds have become a steady source of domestic buying. FPI selling or buying, interest-rate decisions by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), the rupee exchange rate, crude oil prices and global markets often drive broad moves.
