# Technical indicators

Technical analysis studies price and volume history to describe trend and momentum. It describes what the price has done; it does not reliably predict what it will do. Indicators work best as context alongside fundamentals and news, never as a guarantee.

## Moving averages (SMA)

A simple moving average (SMA) is the average closing price over the last N trading days. It smooths out daily noise.

- The 20-day SMA reflects the short-term trend (about one month).
- The 50-day SMA reflects the medium-term trend (about one quarter).
- The 200-day SMA reflects the long-term trend (about one year).

Price above its 200-day SMA is commonly read as a long-term uptrend; below it, a downtrend. When the price crosses its moving average, the trend may be changing, but false signals are common in sideways markets.

## Golden cross and death cross

A golden cross happens when the 50-day SMA rises above the 200-day SMA, which traders read as a bullish shift in trend. A death cross is the opposite: the 50-day SMA falls below the 200-day SMA, read as bearish. Both are lagging signals, because moving averages react only after the price has already moved.

## Relative Strength Index (RSI)

RSI measures the speed of recent price changes on a scale of 0 to 100, usually over 14 days.

- Above 70 is called overbought: the price has risen fast and may pause or pull back.
- Below 30 is called oversold: the price has fallen fast and may bounce.
- Between 40 and 60 is neutral momentum.

In a strong trend RSI can stay overbought or oversold for weeks, so an RSI of 75 is not on its own a reason to expect a fall.

## Volatility

Annualised volatility is the standard deviation of daily returns scaled to a year, shown as a percent. It measures how much the price swings. Large, stable companies often have volatility of 15% to 30%; small caps and speculative stocks can exceed 50%. Higher volatility means a wider range of likely outcomes, both up and down.

## Maximum drawdown

Maximum drawdown is the largest fall from a peak to a later low within a period, in percent. A drawdown of -35% means someone who bought at the top was down 35% at the worst point. It shows how painful holding the stock has been.

## Beta

Beta measures how much a stock moves compared with the overall market. A beta of 1 moves in line with the market, 1.5 moves about 50% more, and 0.6 moves less. Beta is calculated from past data and can change.

## Volume

Volume is the number of shares traded. Price moves on high volume are considered more significant than moves on low volume, because more participants agreed with the move. Unusually high volume often follows news such as earnings results.

## Support and resistance

Support is a price level where buying has repeatedly stopped a fall; resistance is a level where selling has repeatedly stopped a rise. The 52-week high and low often act as psychological levels. These levels are approximate zones, not exact prices.

## Limits of technical analysis

Technical signals are probabilistic and often contradict each other. They ignore the business itself, and they can be overwhelmed instantly by news, earnings surprises or market-wide events. Short-term price movement is close to random, so any outlook based on indicators should carry low to medium confidence at most.
