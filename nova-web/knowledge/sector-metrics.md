# Sector-specific metrics

General ratios like P/E and ROE apply to every company, but each industry has its own key numbers. Comparing a company only with peers in the same sector, using that sector's metrics, gives much better insight.

## IT services (for example TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCLTech)

- Revenue growth in constant currency (CC): growth with exchange-rate effects removed, because most revenue is earned in US dollars and euros.
- Deal wins and total contract value (TCV): large new contracts signed, a leading indicator of future revenue.
- Operating (EBIT) margin: affected by wage hikes, employee utilisation and the rupee exchange rate. A weaker rupee usually helps margins.
- Attrition: the percentage of employees leaving per year. High attrition raises hiring and training costs.
- Utilisation: the share of employees working on billable client projects.
- Client concentration and exposure to sectors such as banking and retail, and to regions such as North America and Europe. Spending cuts by clients in those markets slow revenue growth.

## Banks and NBFCs (for example HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, SBI, Bajaj Finance)

- Net interest margin (NIM): the difference between interest earned on loans and interest paid on deposits, as a percent of assets. Higher is better.
- Gross and net NPA (non-performing assets): loans where repayment has stopped for 90 days or more, as a percent of total loans. Lower is better; rising NPAs are a major warning sign.
- Provision coverage ratio (PCR): how much of bad loans the bank has already set aside money for.
- CASA ratio: the share of deposits in low-cost current and savings accounts. A higher CASA ratio means cheaper funding.
- Capital adequacy ratio (CAR or CRAR): capital as a percent of risk-weighted assets; regulators set minimum levels.
- Loan growth and deposit growth.
- Banks are usually valued with P/B and ROA (return on assets) rather than P/E alone.

## FMCG / consumer goods (for example HUL, ITC, Nestle India)

- Volume growth: growth in the quantity sold, separate from price increases. Volume growth shows real demand.
- Rural versus urban demand trends.
- Gross margin: affected by raw-material prices such as palm oil, wheat and packaging.
- Advertising spending as a percent of sales.
- FMCG companies often trade at high P/E ratios because of stable, predictable earnings.

## Automobiles

- Monthly sales volumes, which companies publish at the start of each month.
- Market share within segments (two-wheelers, passenger vehicles, commercial vehicles, tractors).
- Electric vehicle (EV) transition plans.
- Commodity costs such as steel and aluminium.

## Pharmaceuticals

- US generics pricing and new product approvals from the US FDA.
- FDA inspection outcomes: warning letters or import alerts on a plant can hurt revenue.
- Domestic formulations growth and R&D spending as a percent of revenue.

## Metals, oil and gas, and other cyclical sectors

- Earnings swing with global commodity prices, so P/E can look very low at the top of a cycle and very high at the bottom.
- EV/EBITDA and P/B are often more useful than P/E.
- Watch global demand (especially China), inventory levels and government duties.

## Telecom

- ARPU (average revenue per user): the main measure of pricing power.
- Subscriber additions and data usage per user.
- High debt and spectrum costs make debt levels important.
