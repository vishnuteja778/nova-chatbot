# Risk and investing basics

## Risk and return

Higher potential returns come with higher risk. Equities have historically delivered higher long-term returns than fixed deposits or bonds, but with large swings along the way, including falls of 30% to 50% during crashes. Any single stock is far riskier than a diversified index.

## Why stock prediction is hard

Prices react to new information very quickly, and new information is by definition unexpected. This idea is called the efficient market hypothesis: in its common form, it says publicly available information is largely reflected in prices already. Markets are not perfectly efficient, but beating them consistently is very difficult, and most actively managed funds underperform their benchmark index over long periods after fees. Short-term price moves are close to random. Any model or analyst that claims to predict them with high confidence should be treated with suspicion.

## Time horizon

Over days and weeks, prices are driven mostly by news, sentiment and flows. Over many years, prices tend to follow growth in a company's earnings and cash flows. The longer the horizon, the more fundamentals matter and the less daily noise matters.

## Diversification

Diversification means spreading money across many companies, sectors and asset classes so that one failure cannot ruin the portfolio. Index funds and ETFs (for example on the Nifty 50) give instant diversification at low cost.

## Systematic investment plans (SIPs)

A SIP invests a fixed amount at regular intervals, usually monthly, into a mutual fund. It builds discipline and averages the purchase price over time (rupee-cost averaging), so investors buy more units when prices are low and fewer when they are high.

## Common behavioural mistakes

- Overconfidence: trading too often because of a belief in one's ability to time the market.
- Loss aversion: holding losing stocks too long hoping to "get back to even", while selling winners too early.
- Herd behaviour and FOMO: buying because everyone else is, often near a peak.
- Confirmation bias: looking only for information that supports an existing opinion.
- Recency bias: assuming recent trends will continue indefinitely.
- Anchoring: fixating on the price you paid, which the market does not care about.

## Leverage and derivatives

Futures and options (F&O) and margin trading magnify both gains and losses. SEBI studies have found that a large majority of individual traders in equity F&O lose money; a SEBI study covering FY22 to FY24 reported that about 9 out of 10 individual F&O traders made net losses. They are not suitable for beginners.

## Basic risk checks before investing in a stock

- Do I understand how this company makes money?
- Is the company's debt manageable?
- Am I comfortable if this investment falls 30% or more?
- Is this a small part of a diversified portfolio?
- Is my decision based on research, or on a tip or excitement?

## Advice versus research

Research explains facts, risks and scenarios. Advice tells a specific person what to do with their money, based on their goals, income, timeline and risk tolerance. Personalised investment advice should come from a registered professional (in India, a SEBI-registered investment adviser).
